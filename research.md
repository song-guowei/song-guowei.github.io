## Published Work

- **Market-based versus collective self-governance in irrigation systems: Differential effects on infrastructure condition** (with Y. Zhang, M. Chang, and Y. Zhong)  
  *Journal of Environmental Management*, 413, 130438, 2026  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.130438)  
  <details>
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    Irrigation infrastructure is critical for agricultural productivity, yet its maintenance is often underprovided. Two governance models dominate: market-based approaches and collective self-governance. Using village-level panel data from rural China, we employ a difference-in-differences strategy to compare their effects on infrastructure condition. We find that collective self-governance is associated with significantly better infrastructure condition, with the effect most pronounced in villages with strong social capital and active user participation.
  </details>

- **The effect of land transfer platform transactions on cultivated land quality protection: A dual perspective of tenure stability and economies of scale** (with J. Yue and Q. Xiao)  
  *Journal of China Agricultural Resources and Regional Planning*, 47(3), 45-58, 2026 (in Chinese)  
  <details>
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    This study examines how land transfer platforms—formalized land rental markets—affect cultivated land quality in rural China. Using a dual-perspective framework of tenure stability and economies of scale, we find that platform transactions enhance land quality protection by strengthening tenure security and enabling scale-appropriate farming practices. The effect is more pronounced in regions with well-established land markets and higher levels of agricultural commercialization.
  </details>

## Working Papers

- **When the weather turns against the plow: Climate risk and cropland abandonment in rural China** (with Q. Xiao)  
  *Under review at Land Use Policy*  
  <details>
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    This paper investigates whether climate risk—measured by temperature and precipitation variability—drives cropland abandonment in rural China. Using panel data covering thousands of households across multiple provinces, we identify the causal effect of weather shocks on land use decisions and explore the mitigating role of adaptation strategies such as irrigation and crop diversification.
  </details>

## Selected Work in Progress

- **Digital technologies and collective action in irrigation governance**  
  <details>
    <summary>Summary</summary>
    This project examines how digital technologies—such as smart water metering and mobile-based monitoring—reshape collective action dynamics among water users. Preliminary evidence suggests that digitization may enhance monitoring efficiency but could also weaken social capital if not properly designed.
  </details>

- **Climate risk, institutional design, and common-pool resource management**  
  <details>
    <summary>Summary</summary>
    This project investigates the interaction between climate risk and institutional design: do weather shocks strengthen or weaken collective governance? I am exploring this question using a combination of long-term panel data and historical weather records.
  </details>
