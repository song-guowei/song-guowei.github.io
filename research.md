## Published Work

* **Market-based versus collective self-governance in irrigation systems: Differential effects on infrastructure condition** (with Y. Zhang, M. Chang, and Y. Zhong)  
  *Journal of Environmental Management*, 413, 130438, 2026.  
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2026.130438)
  <details>
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    Irrigation infrastructure is critical for agricultural productivity, yet its maintenance is often underprovided. Two governance models dominate: market-based approaches (where users pay for water and maintenance) and collective self-governance (where users jointly manage the system). Using village-level panel data from rural China, we compare the effects of the two governance models on infrastructure condition employing a difference-in-differences strategy. We find that collective self-governance is associated with significantly better infrastructure condition than market-based governance. The effect is heterogeneous: the advantage of collective governance is most pronounced in villages with strong social capital and active user participation. The findings suggest that devolving management authority to local users—rather than relying on market mechanisms—may be more effective for sustaining common-pool resources in contexts with strong community ties.
  </details>

## Working Papers

* **When the weather turns against the plow: Climate risk and cropland abandonment in rural China** (with Q. Xiao)  
  *Under review at Land Use Policy*
  <details>
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    Climate change poses increasing risks to agricultural production. This paper investigates whether and to what extent climate risk—measured by temperature and precipitation variability—drives cropland abandonment in rural China. Using panel data covering thousands of households across multiple provinces, we identify the causal effect of weather shocks on land use decisions and explore the mitigating role of adaptation strategies such as irrigation and crop diversification.
  </details>

## Selected Work in Progress

* **Digital technologies and collective action in irrigation governance**  
  I am extending my irrigation governance research to examine how digital technologies—such as smart water metering and mobile-based monitoring—reshape collective action dynamics among water users. Preliminary evidence suggests that digitization may enhance monitoring efficiency but could also weaken social capital if not properly designed.

* **Climate risk, institutional design, and common-pool resource management**  
  This project investigates the interaction between climate risk and institutional design: do weather shocks strengthen or weaken collective governance? I am exploring this question using a combination of long-term panel data and historical weather records.
